# Following up real estate leads

Agents rarely lose leads to other agents' brilliance. They lose them to:

- **Slow first replies.** Research on online leads (HBR, 2011) found
  companies that replied within an hour were about 7 times as likely to
  qualify the lead as those that waited even an hour longer. Portal buyers
  enquire with several agents at once.
- **Generic replies** ("Thanks for your interest! Let me know if you have
  any questions") that give the lead nothing to answer.
- **Stopping after two attempts.** Most leads who buy or sell in 6–12
  months were "just looking" when they first enquired.
- **Nurture that's all about the agent** ("Just sold!") instead of useful to
  the lead.

This skill drafts replies and sequences that are fast, specific, and easy
to answer. The agent sends them and stays responsible for consent and
licensing rules.

## Step 1: Classify the lead

Ask only for what's missing:

1. **Source**: portal enquiry on a specific listing, open-house sign-in,
   valuation/CMA request, website form, sign call, referral, past client,
   sphere, expired/withdrawn listing (check local rules on contacting these).
2. **What they actually said or did** (paste the enquiry text).
3. **Buyer, seller, both, landlord or tenant.**
4. **Timeframe** if known: now (0–3 months), soon (3–6), later (6–12+), unknown.
5. **Channel and consent**: did they give a phone number and agree to texts?
   (US: TCPA rules on automated texts and calls, plus state rules and
   do-not-call lists. UK: PECR rules on marketing emails and texts. When
   unsure, reply on the channel they used.)
6. **The agent's offer**: what they can send that's genuinely useful
   (comparable sales, an off-market list, a price-drop alert, a moving
   checklist, a local market update).

## Step 2: The first reply (send within minutes)

Rules:
- **Answer their question first.** If they asked "is it still available?",
  say yes/no in the first line.
- **One easy question** that moves things forward, ideally choice-based:
  "Would Thursday after 5 or Saturday morning suit for a viewing?" beats
  "When are you free?"
- **One piece of value** that shows you know the property or area: the
  thing that isn't in the listing ("the garden gets sun until about 7 pm
  in summer", "there's an offer deadline of Monday noon").
- **Short.** Text under 300 characters, email under 120 words.
- Use their name and the property address. Sign with your name, not just
  the brokerage.
- No pressure lines, no fake scarcity ("lots of interest!") unless it's
  true and specific ("we have 6 viewings booked on Saturday").

US buyers: you can answer questions and talk at an open house, but since
17 August 2024 (NAR settlement practice change) MLS participants need a
**written buyer agreement before touring** a home with a buyer, including
live virtual tours. If the next step is a showing, mention it in plain
words: "Before we tour, I'll send a short one-page agreement that sets out
what I do and how I'm paid. It's negotiable, and I'm happy to walk you
through it." Check your state's rules and brokerage forms.

Produce: the text version, the email version, and a 20-second call
opener for when they pick up.

## Step 3: Qualifying (in conversation, not an interrogation)

Pick 3–4 questions for the first conversation, depending on lead type:

**Buyers**
- What's prompting the move, and what's the timing?
- Have you spoken to a lender / mortgage adviser yet? (Offer an introduction
  only if your brokerage allows referrals and disclose any relationship.)
- Do you have a home to sell first? (UK: "Are you in a chain?")
- What would make a home an instant no?

**Sellers / valuation requests**
- What's prompting the sale, and is there a date driving it?
- Where are you moving to, and do you need to buy at the same time?
- What work have you done since you bought? (Feeds the CMA.)
- Have you had other agents round? (Ask lightly; it tells you the competition.)

**Landlords / tenants**: move date, budget or rent expectation, pets,
furnished or not; landlords: compliance status (UK: gas safety, EICR, EPC,
deposit protection; US: local licensing).

Record the answers in a short **lead note** (Step 5).

## Step 4: The follow-up sequence

Choose by timeframe. Each touch must give them something or ask a single
easy question. Change channel if two attempts on one channel go unanswered.

**Hot (0–3 months), no reply yet**
| Day | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Text + email | First reply (Step 2) |
| 0 (+2 h) | Call | If no reply. Leave a 15-second voicemail with one reason to call back. |
| 1 | Text | A similar listing or the answer to a likely question ("The seller has had the boiler serviced; the certificate is in the pack.") |
| 3 | Email | 3 alternatives in their range, one line each on why |
| 6 | Call | Ask about timing |
| 10 | Text | "Still looking, or have you found somewhere?" (gives an easy out) |
| 14 | Email | Move them to the monthly nurture (below), and say so. |

**Warm (3–12 months)**: a call or text in week 1, then every 2–3 weeks
for 3 months: new listings matching their brief, a recent sold price on a
street they like, a "what your budget buys now" note. Then monthly.

**Sellers not ready yet**: send an initial pricing range only with a CMA
(see `cma-summary` if available); then quarterly: what sold near them and
for how much, one improvement worth doing before listing, a seasonal
timing note.

**Cold / past clients / sphere (monthly nurture)**: one genuinely useful
email per month (local market numbers with the source, a home-maintenance
checklist for the season, a local business or event), plus a personal
touch on the purchase anniversary. Never more than one sales message in four.

**Stop rules**: stop and remove them when they ask, when they've bought or
sold with someone else (send a gracious congratulations), or when they
show no engagement for 12 months (one final "should I keep sending?" message).

## Quality checklist (run before answering)

- [ ] The first line answers what they asked.
- [ ] Exactly one easy question, ideally with options.
- [ ] One specific piece of value that isn't in the listing.
- [ ] Text under 300 characters; email under 120 words.
- [ ] No fake scarcity, pressure or claims the agent can't back.
- [ ] US buyer showing: the written buyer agreement is mentioned plainly.
- [ ] Channel matches the consent given; opt-out respected in sequences.
- [ ] Every follow-up touch gives value or asks one question; channels vary.
- [ ] End with "Check before you send": facts to verify (availability, dates), consent, brokerage rules.

## Example

User: "Zillow lead, Maria, asked 'is 14 Oak Lane still available? can we see
it this weekend?' at 9:40pm. Yes it's available, open house Sat 1-3, two
other showings booked Sunday. She gave a phone number. Write the reply."

Good output: a text, "Hi Maria, yes, 14 Oak Lane is still available. There's
an open house Saturday 1–3, or I can show you privately Sunday at 11 or 4.
Which suits? (The back deck gets the afternoon sun, so 4 is a nice time.)
– Sam, Brightway Realty", an email version with the open house details and a
note about the short buyer agreement before a private tour, a call opener,
and the "hot lead" sequence with dates filled in from Saturday.
